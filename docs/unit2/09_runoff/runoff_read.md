# Pre-Class - Runoff

This lesson covers surface runoff and the storm hydrograph: the parts of a hydrograph and what controls its shape, rainfall excess and infiltration losses, baseflow separation, recession analysis, and how to compute the volume of direct runoff from a measured hydrograph and express it as a depth over the watershed.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Sections 1.6 (Streamflow and the Hydrograph) and 1.7 (Hydrograph Analysis).

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - What a Storm Hydrograph Is and Its Parts

In one or two sentences, define what a storm hydrograph is and identify its three main shape segments. Explain what physically governs the overall shape and timing.

### Question 2 - Rainfall Excess, Infiltration, and Direct Runoff

Write the simplified rainfall-excess equation in one or two sentences, define each term, and explain how rainfall excess (Pn) is related to the direct-runoff hydrograph (DRO).

### Question 3 - Baseflow and Baseflow Separation Methods

In one or two sentences, define baseflow, explain why we separate it from the total hydrograph, and list the three common separation methods.

### Question 4 - Recession Analysis and the Recession Constant k

Write the recession equation, define each variable, and explain in one or two sentences what the recession constant k tells you about the watershed.

### Question 5 - Computing Runoff Volume from a Hydrograph (Watershed Inches)

In one or two sentences, describe how to compute the volume of direct runoff from a measured discharge hydrograph and convert that volume to a depth (inches) over the watershed; explain why the convenient unit "ac-in" arises.
