# Pre-Class - Flow Frequency

This lesson covers flood frequency analysis: return period and annual exceedance probability, empirical plotting positions (Weibull), fitted distributions for annual peaks (Gumbel and Log-Pearson Type III), the risk that a design event is exceeded during a project's life, and how a flow-duration curve differs from a return-period analysis.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Sections 3.4 (Return Period or Recurrence Interval) and 3.5 (Common Probabilistic Models).

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - Return Period and Annual Exceedance Probability

Define return period T in one sentence and write the relationship between T and the annual exceedance probability (AEP). Then explain in one sentence why labeling the 100-year flood as the "1% annual chance flood" is sometimes preferred when communicating with the public.

### Question 2 - Weibull Plotting Position (Empirical AEP)

Write the Weibull plotting-position formula, identify each variable, and explain in one sentence why this is called a non-parametric method.

### Question 3 - Risk Over a Design Life

Write the equation for the risk that a T-year event will occur at least once during an n-year project life, and use it to compute the risk that the 100-yr flood occurs at least once during a 30-year design life.

### Question 4 - Gumbel Method of Moments for Annual Peaks

Write the Gumbel quantile equation (in mean/standard-deviation form) used to estimate the T-year peak from an annual maximum series, identify each variable, and state in one sentence why the Gumbel distribution is a reasonable choice for annual maximum flows.

### Question 5 - Return Periods vs. Flow-Duration Curves

In one or two sentences, explain the difference between a return-period analysis (e.g., the 100-yr flood) and a flow-duration curve, including which series of flows each one uses and what question each one answers.
