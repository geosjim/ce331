## Description

Waters of the earth, their occurrence, circulation, and distribution. Relationships among precipitation, evaporation, infiltration, transpiration, groundwater, and stream runoff.

CE 331 introduces the fundamentals of hydrologic data collection, analysis, and use in examining water balances in the natural and urban hydrologic cycles. The course provides the basic foundation for working in engineering firms that carry out hydrologic and hydraulic analysis and design.

## Course Purpose

By applying principles of the hydrologic cycle, students in this class will become responsible stewards of water resources with the ability to solve current and emerging challenges.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the semester, you should be able to:

1. **Steward water resources.** See engineering as caretakers of our precious water resources in a way that blesses our neighbors.
2. **Trust your data.** Evaluate hydrologic data sources for accuracy, authority, and trustworthiness, and use them for relevant problems.
3. **Solve engineering problems.** Identify, formulate, and solve hydrologic engineering problems.
4. **Track water through the hydrologic cycle.** Apply mathematical, observational, and data-driven methods to evaluate water movement across natural systems and the built environment.
5. **Weigh uncertainty.** Understand the limitations of hydrologic analysis and the environmental, social, and economic risks that follow, and make decisions amid uncertainty.
6. **Justify your solutions.** Defend hydrologic analysis and management solutions in the context of sound engineering, practical, and societal interests.

## Classroom Procedures

In the first half of class we will review the class objectives and the pre-class questions covering the underlying concepts. In the second half we will work sample problems that are very similar to the assigned homework. We may not complete all of these problems in class, depending on the time spent in review, but we will at least review the setup and general flow so that working the practice problems will be much more effective.

## Study Habits

I am placing a premium on the pre-class preparation: reviewing the text and responding to the questions carries the same weight as the practice problems themselves. Expect to spend about 1 hour reviewing the materials, responding to the questions, and coming with questions that we can cover during the first half of class, guided by the in-class presentation, which you also have access to as you prepare.

Pre-class preparation is due by **8:00 am the morning of class**, when we will discuss the material, so plan your time accordingly. You are welcome to work ahead, but I don't recommend going too far: the point is not just answering the questions but being prepared in class, so that we can move from understanding the concepts to working practice problems. Doing this makes the problem solving faster and easier to keep current, so that you are prepared for the individual exams on each of the major chapters we cover.

## Homework

Homework is generally due at 5:00 pm on the day of the following class. Give succinct, clear answers and show the development of your solution. Each problem is scored on a three-point scale:

| Score | Meaning |
|:---:|---|
| 0 | Nothing submitted |
| 1 | You made an intelligent attempt |
| 2 | You show good thought and process, but with some errors |
| 3 | You show good thought and process and reach the correct answer |

Hydrology is part art and part science, so there is sometimes more than one solution, and different assumptions can lead to different answers, including those in the keys the TAs follow. If you believe you made correct assumptions that lead to a different answer, follow up with the TAs or the instructor.

**Late homework receives a flat 20% deduction.** If you find yourself getting significantly behind, come and talk with me before it becomes overwhelming, and we will work out the best way to get back on track.
