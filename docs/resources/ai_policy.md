# Course AI Use Policy

*Guided by the ETHIC Framework for Responsible AI (EYARC Experience platform, AI Ethics assignment, EY Foundation)*

Artificial intelligence (AI) tools are part of how we learn, teach, and work in this course. Used well, they can deepen understanding and free us to focus on the ideas that matter most. Used carelessly, they can undermine learning, fairness, and trust. This policy explains how AI will be used in the course, both by the instructor and teaching assistants and by you as students, and the expectations that apply to everyone. Our shared standard is guided by the ETHIC framework: Educational Value, Transparency, Honesty and Integrity, Individual Accountability, and Consciousness of Bias and Fairness.

## The ETHIC Principles

**E - Educational Value.** AI tools should be used to enhance learning and skill-building, not as a substitute for personal effort or engagement. In this course, AI is expected to support your growth in helping you practice, explore, and refine your thinking rather than replacing the critical thinking and learning experiences that are the point of the class.

**T - Transparency.** Be open about when and how AI is used in your work. Whether it appears in research, data processing, content generation, or decision-making, others should be aware when AI has played a role. I commit to this same standard and will disclose course uses of AI below.

**H - Honesty and Integrity.** Avoid using AI in ways that deceive others or misrepresent your work. Integrity requires that AI tools are applied in a way that maintains the authenticity of your output and avoids shortcuts that compromise academic and ethical standards.

**I - Individual Accountability.** Even when using AI, the individual remains accountable for the outcome. All AI-assisted work must be accurate and appropriate, and you take full responsibility for anything you submit, including any errors, fabricated sources, or flawed reasoning an AI tool may introduce.

**C - Consciousness of Bias and Fairness.** Stay mindful of potential biases in AI outputs. Make deliberate efforts to avoid perpetuating discrimination or unfair practices. In this course, that means AI-influenced decisions, including assessment, are reviewed by people to guard against bias and to ensure fairness for every student.

## How I Am Using AI in This Course

In keeping with the Transparency principle, I want you to know exactly where AI is used in the design and running of this course. AI is a tool I use to work more efficiently, but the responsibility for its output is mine.

**Generating questions and answers.** I use AI to help draft course questions and answer keys. This includes the pre-class preparation questions tied to each lecture's reading and learning objectives, the more complete model answers and short sample responses posted in Learning Suite after you submit, assignment and homework problems, and exam questions. I do not accept AI output as-is. Every AI-assisted question and answer is reviewed and iterated on by me and/or the TA to confirm that it is accurate, clearly worded, appropriately challenging, and aligned with what we have covered in class. Because I am thoroughly acquainted with the material I can catch and correct errors, and am able to revise or discard questions and answers as needed.

As with non-AI produced materials by myself or from the text and answer keys, there still may be errors from time to time that have not been caught, and I welcome your feedback to correct and improve them as they are discovered.

**In-class problems and homework setups.** I also use AI to help me prepare the worked sample problems we solve together in class and the setups that accompany each homework assignment. These are built from the existing homework problems and solution keys and have been checked and reworked by a TA and reviewed by me. The in-class examples reinforce the same skills you will practice through homework.

**Grading and feedback.** I may use AI to assist with grading and with drafting feedback on your work, but it is never the final word on your grade. AI-assisted grading and feedback is checked for the same reason I have always had TAs check and follow up: to ensure accuracy within the reasonable amount of time and resources available for grading. Where AI and human judgment disagree, human judgment prevails, including your own review of the feedback. If you ever believe a grade or piece of feedback is mistaken, please raise it with a TA or me and we will review and adjust.

If you have questions about how AI is used in any part of this course, please ask. Transparency runs both ways.

## Expectations for Student Use of AI

You are encouraged to use AI as a learning aid, following the ETHIC principles outlined above. When in doubt, ask before you use it, and always be prepared to explain and stand behind your own work. You will need that on exams.

**Generally acceptable uses**

- Brainstorming ideas, outlining, or exploring different approaches to a problem.
- Explaining concepts, working through practice problems, or checking your understanding.
- Summarizing readings or generating study questions to test yourself.

**Generally unacceptable uses**

- Submitting AI-generated work as your own without the effort, understanding, or disclosure.
- Pasting a pre-class preparation question into an AI tool and submitting its answer. The value of pre-class work is your own thinking before class; a generic AI answer defeats that purpose and will not prepare you for problem-solving in class or on exams.
- Using AI on any assignment, quiz, or exam where its use has been explicitly prohibited.
- Using AI in any way that misrepresents your knowledge, deceives the instructor or peers, or otherwise violates academic integrity.

## Disclosure and Accountability

When you use AI in work you submit, disclose it briefly. Note which tool you used and how it helped (for example, in a short line at the end of the assignment). Disclosing AI use is not an admission of wrongdoing; it is what integrity requires. You remain fully accountable for everything you submit, including any inaccuracies AI introduces.

AI is prohibited for all exams, and some assignments may have rules that are more or less permissive than this general policy. Where an assignment's instructions differ, those instructions control. If the rules for a given task are unclear, ask before proceeding.

## A Note on Fairness

Because AI systems can reflect and amplify bias, any use of AI that touches assessment in this course is paired with human review. My commitment and that of the teaching assistants is that your work is evaluated accurately and fairly, and that you are always able to reach a person about any decision that affects your grade. If you have concerns about fairness, accessibility, or the use of AI at any point, please contact me.

This policy is part of the course syllabus. It may be updated during the term; students will be notified of any changes.
