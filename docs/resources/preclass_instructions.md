# Pre-Class Preparation

Before each class you will answer a short set of questions in a Learning Suite quiz, based on the assigned reading and the lecture presentation slides, with a focus on the learning objectives listed in those presentations. Each lesson's **Pre-Class** page lists the reading, links the slides and shows the questions.

Plan on spending 30-60 minutes reviewing the reading and the presentation, and another 20-30 minutes responding. I am not looking for full and complete understanding at this point. I want to know what you think: a couple of sentences with any reasonable answer will be given credit. The bottom line is that I'm looking for evidence that you reviewed the material and have ideas and questions, not final answers.

Pre-class assignments carry the same weight as homework, and they are really the thing to focus on. Coming to class familiar with the concepts means we can spend 15-20 minutes reviewing the objectives and most of our time together solving problems. If you haven't done the pre-class work, you will be somewhat lost in the workout problems, which are practice for the exams.

## Scoring

Pre-class work is scored on preparation and an honest attempt at telling me what you think, not on getting everything right. I expect you to be uncertain about the material, and most answers will not be complete. Each question earns credit when it responds with genuine effort that shows you did the reading. A blank, nonsense or off-topic answer earns no credit. The two lowest pre-class scores are dropped.

As soon as you submit, Learning Suite posts a complete answer. Compare it with your own responses before class. This is one of the most important learning steps: see what you understood, clarify what you didn't, and bring additional questions to class. I will use the presentation to help answer questions, but I will not repeat all of it as a lecture, since I expect you to already have a basic understanding.

## Deadline

Assignments are due at **8:00 am on the day of class** and close at that time. Don't wait until just before class if you can help it: having time to review your answers before class is extremely helpful. Because the purpose is preparation, late submissions are not accepted for credit. You can still submit afterwards to see the complete answers for study.

## AI and Honesty

The value here comes from your thinking before class. Pasting a question into an AI tool and submitting its answer defeats the purpose and won't prepare you for exams or homework. Write your own answer first, in your own words; that answer is what earns credit. If you then use an AI tool to critique or expand your answer, add that after your own answer and say which tool you used. See the [Course AI Use Policy](ai_policy.md).
