# 1.1 Demographics and Water Consumption

## Census and Demographics

You can obtain the most recent population counts from the US Census. Search for city level population based on the 2020 census coming from a census.gov website. Links and website layout change often so you will need to search for them.

- [https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/popest/2020s-total-cities-and-towns.html](https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/popest/2020s-total-cities-and-towns.html){target='blank'}

Official population projections for the future, rather than current counts, come from different groups in each state. In Utah they are made by the Kem C. Gardner Institute and the University of Utah.

- [https://gardner.utah.edu/utah-demographics/population-projections/utah-2065-long-term-vintage-2025/](https://gardner.utah.edu/utah-demographics/population-projections/utah-2065-long-term-vintage-2025/){target='blank'}

## Consumption data

The EPA requires public water systems to provide public reports. You can find information about consumption rates and sources in a water "management plan" or "conservation plan". These are supposed to be updated at least every 5 years and are on a Public Works website.

For national consumption trend information, check with the USGS (circular 1441) and the EPA.

- [https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/water-use-united-states](https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/water-use-united-states){target='blank'}
- [https://www.usgs.gov/tools/national-water-availability-assessment-data-companion](https://www.usgs.gov/tools/national-water-availability-assessment-data-companion){target='blank'}
- [https://www.epa.gov/watersense/learn-more](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/learn-more){target='blank'}
