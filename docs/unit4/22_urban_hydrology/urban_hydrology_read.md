# Pre-Class - Urban Hydrology

Urbanization changes how a watershed responds to rain. This lesson covers how development alters the runoff hydrograph, the minor and major drainage systems in an urban catchment, and the Rational Method for peak flow: the equation and its units, why the storm duration is set equal to the time of concentration, and why the directly connected impervious area (DCIA) must be checked along with the whole catchment.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Sections 6.1 (Characteristics of Urban Hydrology) and 6.4 (Methods for Quantity Analysis).

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - How Urbanization Alters the Hydrologic Cycle

In one or two sentences, describe how urbanizing a watershed changes its runoff hydrograph compared to the pre-development (rural) condition, and explain the single most important physical reason for the change.

### Question 2 - Minor vs. Major Drainage Systems

Define the minor drainage system and the major drainage system in an urban catchment, and explain why the major system must be designed even though it is rarely used.

### Question 3 - The Rational Method Equation

Write out the Rational Method equation in U.S. customary units, identify each variable and its units, and explain in one sentence why the conversion constant is approximately 1.

### Question 4 - Time of Concentration and Rainfall Intensity

In one or two sentences, explain why the Rational Method uses a storm duration equal to the time of concentration (tr = tc) and not some shorter or longer duration.

### Question 5 - DCIA Check and Why the Method Becomes Conservative

In one or two sentences, explain what Directly Connected Impervious Area (DCIA) is, why an engineer must always check the DCIA-only peak flow in addition to the whole-catchment peak flow, and why the Rational Method becomes conservative (over-predicts) as a basin gets larger.
