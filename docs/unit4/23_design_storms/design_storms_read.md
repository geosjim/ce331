# Pre-Class - Design Storms

Urban drainage design starts with a design rainfall. This lesson covers the time of concentration and how TR-55 computes it from sheet flow, shallow concentrated flow, and channel flow segments; why tc sets the design rainfall duration; how to use (and misuse) IDF curves; and how synthetic design storms such as the SCS Type II 24-hr storm are built.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Sections 6.2 (Review of Physical Processes) and 6.3 (Rainfall Analysis in Urban Basins).

USDA NRCS, *Urban Hydrology for Small Watersheds* (TR-55), Chapter 3 (Time of Concentration and Travel Time): [TR-55 Manual.pdf](TR-55%20Manual.pdf)

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - What Time of Concentration Really Means

Define time of concentration (tc) in one sentence, give the two equivalent physical definitions, and explain why tc is NOT the travel time of a parcel of water down the catchment.

### Question 2 - TR-55 Flow Segments

In one or two sentences, name the three flow regimes used in the TR-55 method to compute tc, give a brief description of each, and state the 300-ft rule for sheet flow.

### Question 3 - Why tc Sets the Design Rainfall Duration

In one or two sentences, explain why the Rational Method (and IDF-based design in general) uses a rainfall duration equal to tc, and what error would result from using a much longer or much shorter duration.

### Question 4 - Use and Misuse of IDF Curves

In one or two sentences, state what an IDF curve represents, give the correct way to use it, and name one common misuse described in Section 6.3.

### Question 5 - Synthetic Design Storms

In one or two sentences, describe what a synthetic design storm is, list the standard steps used to construct one (e.g., an SCS Type II 24-hr storm), and state one important caveat about the return period of the resulting hyetograph.
