# Pre-Class - Reservoir Routing

Reservoir routing tracks a flood hydrograph through a reservoir or detention basin. This lesson applies the continuity equation with the level-pool assumption, develops the storage-indication (Modified Puls) form of the routing equation and the storage-indication curve built from stage-storage-discharge data, and compares reservoir routing with Muskingum river routing and full hydraulic routing.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Sections 4.1 (Hydrologic and Hydraulic Routing) and 4.3 (Hydrologic Reservoir Routing).

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - Purpose of Reservoir Routing and the Continuity Equation

In one or two sentences, explain what "routing" accomplishes for a reservoir or detention basin and write the basic form of the continuity equation that governs it. Identify each term.

### Question 2 - Level-Pool Assumption and Storage-Discharge Relationships

What is the "level-pool" assumption, and why does it allow us to write both storage and outflow as single-valued functions of one variable (water-surface elevation H)?

### Question 3 - The Storage-Indication (Modified Puls) Equation

Write the storage-indication form of the continuity equation (Eq. 4-13 in the text), explain which terms are known and which are unknown at each time step, and describe in one sentence the algebraic trick that gets both unknowns onto one side.

### Question 4 - The Storage-Indication Curve

Describe in one or two sentences how the storage-indication curve is constructed from the stage–storage–discharge data, and explain why the curve depends on the routing time step Δt.

### Question 5 - Reservoir Routing vs. River Routing and Hydraulic Routing

In one or two sentences, contrast hydrologic reservoir routing (Modified Puls) with hydrologic river routing (Muskingum) and with full hydraulic routing (Saint-Venant). When is reservoir routing the correct choice?
