# Pre-Class - Darcy's Law

This lesson starts the groundwater unit. It covers the vertical distribution of subsurface water, unconfined and confined aquifers, porosity and hydraulic conductivity, and Darcy's Law for computing groundwater velocity and flow rate. It also distinguishes the Darcy velocity from the seepage velocity, which is the one used for contaminant travel time.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Sections 8.1 (Introduction), 8.2 (Properties of Ground Water) and 8.3 (Ground Water Movement).

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - Aquifer Types and Vertical Distribution of Subsurface Water

In one or two sentences describe the vertical distribution of subsurface water from the ground surface down through the saturated zone, and distinguish among an unconfined aquifer, a confined aquifer, and an aquitard.

### Question 2 - Darcy's Law

Write out Darcy's Law for one-dimensional flow, identify each variable and its units, and explain the meaning of the negative sign.

### Question 3 - Hydraulic Conductivity

In one or two sentences, explain what hydraulic conductivity K represents, give the approximate range of K values spanned from clean gravel to clay (in cm/s).

### Question 4 - Darcy Velocity vs. Seepage Velocity

In one or two sentences, distinguish between Darcy velocity V and seepage velocity Vs, write the equation that connects them, and explain why seepage velocity (not Darcy velocity) must be used when estimating contaminant travel time.
