# Example A - Darcy's Law in 1-D

A horizontal sand-filled column in the fluids-lab constant-head permeameter delivers a steady discharge through a well-sorted medium sand. You measure the head loss between the two piezometers and the cross-sectional area of the column, and are given the porosity from a specific-yield test.

**Given**

- K = 5 × 10⁻³ cm/s (medium sand)
- dh/dL = 0.008 (dimensionless)
- A = 50 cm² (column cross-section)
- n = 0.30 (effective porosity)

**Find**

a. Darcy velocity V (cm/s and m/day)<br>
b. Seepage velocity Vs (cm/s and m/day)<br>
c. Volumetric discharge Q (cm³/s and L/day)<br>
d. Which velocity would you use to estimate the arrival time of a dissolved tracer at the downstream end of the column?

# Example B - Confined-Aquifer Flow

A confined sandstone aquifer beneath an agricultural valley has uniform thickness and width. Two monitoring wells drilled through the confining layers measure the piezometric head at points 1 and 2 aligned with the flow direction.

**Given**

- Aquifer thickness b = 30 m
- Width perpendicular to flow w = 800 m
- Well 1: h₁ = 60.0 m
- Well 2: h₂ = 55.5 m (downgradient)
- Distance between wells L = 1000 m
- K = 1.2 m/day

**Find**

a. Total daily flow through the aquifer, Q (m³/day)<br>
b. Head z at a point 400 m from Well 1 and 600 m from Well 2

Hint (b): the piezometric surface in a uniform confined aquifer varies linearly with x, so interpolate between h₁ and h₂.

# Example C - Constant-Head Permeameter

Solve Darcy's Law for K when Q, A, L and h are measured.

**Given**

- Sample length L = 15 cm
- Cross-sectional area A = 20 cm²
- Applied head h = 8 cm
- Volume collected V = 50 mL
- Collection time t = 20 min = 1200 s

**Find**

Hydraulic conductivity K (report in cm/s), using the constant-head formula

$$K = \frac{V L}{A t h}$$

State the material class implied by your K value (Table 8-3).
