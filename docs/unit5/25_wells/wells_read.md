# Pre-Class - Wells

This lesson extends Darcy's Law to unconfined aquifers and to flow toward wells. It covers the Dupuit assumptions and the Dupuit parabola for one-dimensional unconfined flow, the steady-state well equations for confined and unconfined aquifers, and the use of superposition of well drawdowns to design a construction dewatering scheme.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Sections 8.6 (Dupuit Equation) and 8.9 (Steady-State Well Hydraulics).

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - The Dupuit Assumptions and Why We Need Them

In one or two sentences, state the three Dupuit assumptions used to analyze flow in an unconfined aquifer, and explain why we cannot just apply Darcy's Law directly the way we do in a confined aquifer.

### Question 2 - Steady Radial Flow to a Well in a Confined Aquifer

Write the Thiem equation for steady radial flow to a fully penetrating well in a confined aquifer with two observation wells and identify each variable.

### Question 3 - Steady Radial Flow to a Well in an Unconfined Aquifer

Write the steady-state well equation for an unconfined aquifer (Equation 8-42), and identify each variable.

### Question 4 - Superposition and Construction Dewatering

In one or two sentences, explain the principle of superposition for multi-well systems, describe how it is used to design a construction dewatering scheme, and state one assumption that must hold for superposition to be valid.
