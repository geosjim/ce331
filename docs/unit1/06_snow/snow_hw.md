## Problem 1 - Snowpack SWE and Runoff Volume

A 12 mi² mountain basin has average snow depth = 120 cm and density = 300 kg/m³.

a) Compute the snow water equivalent (SWE) in mm and inches.<br>
b) If all snow melts and becomes runoff (ignore losses), estimate the total runoff volume in acre-ft.

$$SWE = 0.01 \cdot d_s \cdot \rho_s$$

## Problem 2 - Degree-Day Melt by Elevation Band

A basin has two elevation zones: 60% of area at 2000 m (mean air temperature = +4 °C) and 40% at 2600 m. Assume a temperature lapse rate of 1 °C per 100 m, base melt temperature $T_B$ = 0 °C, and snow density $\rho_s$ = 350 kg/m³.

Compute: (i) daily melt (mm) in each zone, (ii) basin-average daily melt (mm), and (iii) total over 3 days (assume temperatures constant).

$$M = D_f (T_i - T_B)$$

$$D_f = 0.011 \cdot \rho_s \quad \text{(mm/day·°C)}$$

## Problem 3 - Effect of Albedo on Daily Melt (Energy Shortcut)

Incoming shortwave at the snow surface is 500 ly/day. Compare melt for (1) fresh snow (albedo α = 0.8) and (2) aged snow (α = 0.5). Assume other fluxes (net longwave + sensible + latent + ground + rain) sum to +60 ly/day in both cases, and assume ripe snow. Compute daily melt (in/day) using the shortcut.

$$Q_n = (1 - \alpha) \cdot 500 + 60 \quad \text{(ly/day)}$$

$$M = \Delta H / 80 \quad \text{(cm/day)}$$

## Problem 4 - Snow Line, Areal Coverage and Basin Melt

A basin area-elevation distribution is: 20% < 1800 m, 50% between 1800-2200 m, and 30% > 2200 m. During a storm, the freezing level is at 2000 m; assume precipitation fell as snow above 2000 m and rain below.

The next day, the index station at 2000 m has mean daily air temperature +3 °C. Use $D_f$ = 3.0 mm/day·°C and $T_B$ = 0 °C.

Estimate: (i) the fraction of the basin that is snow-covered, and (ii) the basin-average daily melt (mm/day).
