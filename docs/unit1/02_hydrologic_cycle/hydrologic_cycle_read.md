# Pre-Class - Hydrologic Cycle

This lesson introduces the hydrologic cycle: the pathways precipitation follows once it reaches the land surface, the watershed as the basic unit of hydrologic analysis, and the continuity equation that ties inflows, outflows and storage together in a water balance. It also introduces the key unit conversion in hydrology, expressing a volume of water as a depth over a watershed.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Sections 1.1 (Introduction to Hydrology) and 1.4 (The Hydrologic Cycle).

In one or two sentences, name and briefly describe the main pathways that precipitation follows once it hits the land surface, and identify the single 'driver' that keeps the cycle moving.

### Question 2 - The Watershed as the Basic Hydrologic Unit

Define a watershed in one sentence, and explain in one or two more sentences why hydrologists organize essentially all analysis, measurement, and modeling around the watershed (rather than, say, political boundaries or grid cells).

### Question 3 - The Continuity Equation and the Watershed Water Balance

### Question 4 - Depth vs. Volume: The Key Unit Conversion in Hydrology

In one or two sentences, explain why hydrologists usually report precipitation and runoff as a 'depth' (in. or mm) rather than a volume (ac-ft or m³), and describe the arithmetic step needed to convert a streamflow measurement (cfs) into an equivalent depth over the watershed.
