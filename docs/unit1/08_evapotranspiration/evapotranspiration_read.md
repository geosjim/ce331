# Pre-Class - Evapotranspiration

This lesson extends evaporation to evapotranspiration (ET), the combined loss of water from soil and plants. It covers potential vs. actual ET and the factors that limit ET, the temperature-based Blaney-Criddle method with crop coefficients, the Penman-Monteith method, and OpenET, a satellite-based dataset of field-scale ET.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Section 2.6 (Hydrologic Loss - Evapotranspiration), and review the Blaney-Criddle handouts below.

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - Evaporation vs. Transpiration vs. Evapotranspiration

Define evapotranspiration in one sentence and explain why hydrologists usually report ET as a single combined quantity rather than separating evaporation and transpiration.

### Question 2 - Potential vs. Actual ET

In one or two sentences, explain the difference between potential ET (PET) and actual ET (AET) and identify the main physical reason AET often falls below PET in late summer.

### Question 3 - The Blaney-Criddle Equation

Write out the Blaney-Criddle equation for monthly potential consumptive use and the modification used for crop ET, identify each variable and its units, and state one reason this temperature-based method is still used today.

### Question 4 - The Crop Coefficient Kc

In one or two sentences, explain what the crop coefficient Kc represents physically, why it varies through the growing season, and what kinds of values it typically takes.

### Question 5 - OpenET and Why Modern Datasets Matter

In one or two sentences, describe what OpenET (etdata.org) provides and explain one practical advantage it offers over a temperature-based estimate like Blaney-Criddle.
