# Pre-Class - Water Balances

This lesson applies the hydrologic water-balance equation to watersheds, using rainfall, evaporation and runoff expressed either as a flow rate times time or as a depth over the watershed area. It also introduces the streamflow hydrograph and how development changes it, and the Rational Method for estimating peak flow from a small watershed.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Sections 1.4 (The Hydrologic Cycle), 1.5 (Simple Rainfall-Runoff) and 1.6 (Streamflow and the Hydrograph).

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - The Watershed

Define a "watershed" in your own words, and explain why the textbook calls it the basic hydrologic unit for measurement and analysis.

### Question 2 - The Water Balance Equation

The hydrologic water-balance equation is given as P - R - G - E - T = ΔS. Identify what each letter stands for, and state which terms are inflows, which are outflows, and what ΔS represents.

### Question 3 - Two Ways of Expressing Volume

The reading shows that the volume of water can be written either as flow rate × time or as depth × area. Why is expressing rainfall, evaporation, and runoff as a "depth over the watershed" (inches or mm) useful when comparing terms in a water budget?

### Question 4 - The Rational Method

Write out the Rational Method formula Qₚ = CiA, identify the units of each variable, and state one key assumption that must be true for the method to apply.

### Question 5 - Hydrograph and Urbanization

Looking at Figure 1-21, describe how urbanization (development) typically changes a watershed's hydrograph compared to its natural condition. Specifically, what happens to the peak flow and the time to peak, and why?
