# Pre-Class - Evaporation

This lesson covers evaporation from open water: why it matters little in a single storm but a great deal in long-term water budgets, the physical variables that control it, and the main estimation methods: the water-budget (mass balance) method, the mass-transfer (aerodynamic) method, the energy method, pan evaporation with a pan coefficient, and the combined Penman method.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Section 2.6 (Hydrologic Loss - Evaporation).

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - Why Evaporation Matters

Evaporation is small during a single storm but dominant in long-term water balances. In one or two sentences, explain why we mostly ignore it for storm-event design but always include it for annual or seasonal water-resources planning.

### Question 2 - Driving Variables

Name the four physical variables that most strongly control the rate of evaporation from an open-water surface, and briefly explain the role of each.

### Question 3 - Water-Budget Method

Write the basic water-budget form used to back evaporation out of measured terms over a lake and identify each variable. State one practical limitation.

### Question 4 - Mass-Transfer (Aerodynamic) Method

State the mass-transfer (Dalton-type) equation for evaporation, identify each variable with its units, and explain why wind speed appears as a multiplier.

### Question 5 - Pan Evaporation and the Pan Coefficient

In one or two sentences, explain what a Class A evaporation pan measures, why a pan coefficient Kp is needed to convert pan readings to lake evaporation, and roughly what range Kp covers.
