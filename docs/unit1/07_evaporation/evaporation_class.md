# Example A - Daily Lake Evaporation from the Penman Nomograph

A shallow lake has the following observed conditions:

- Mean daily air temperature: 20 °C
- Daily solar radiation: 450 cal/cm²
- Mean daily dew point: 10 °C
- Wind movement (6 in. above pan): 4.0 ft/s

Use Figure 2-14 in the textbook (the four-quadrant Penman nomograph) to estimate daily evaporation in inches. The figure uses inputs in °F, cal/cm², and mi/day, so you will need to convert first.

**Find**

a. Inputs converted to nomograph units<br>
b. Daily evaporation (in/day)

# Example B - Pan Evaporation: 7-Day Class A Record

A Class A pan is maintained next to a small reservoir. Water level is read daily; rainfall is also recorded. The pan coefficient is $K_p$ = 0.70.

| Day | Rainfall (in) | Level (in) |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 0 | 8.00 |
| 2 | 0.10 | 7.85 |
| 3 | 0 | 7.65 |
| 4 | 0.30 | 7.70 |
| 5 | 0 | 7.55 |
| 6 | 0.05 | 7.45 |
| 7 | 0 | 7.30 |

For each day, compute the daily pan evaporation, then the corresponding lake evaporation. Report the 6-day total and the average daily lake evaporation.

**Given**

- Pan E = (previous level + rain) - today's level
- Lake E = $K_p$ · Pan E

**Find**

a. Daily pan E and lake E (in/day)<br>
b. 6-day total lake E (in)<br>
c. Average lake E (in/day)
