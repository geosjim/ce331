# Pre-Class - Precipitation

This lesson covers how precipitation varies in space and time and how it is measured and summarized: cumulative rainfall plots and hyetographs, averaging point gauge records over a watershed with the arithmetic mean and Thiessen polygons, radar and satellite estimates, and Intensity-Duration-Frequency (IDF) curves such as NOAA Atlas 14 for choosing a design rainfall.

## Reading

Bedient, Huber & Vieux, *Hydrology and Floodplain Analysis*, 6th ed., Section 1.3 (Precipitation).

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - Spatial and Temporal Variability of Precipitation

Annual rainfall in the contiguous U.S. ranges from a few inches to more than 100 inches. In one or two sentences, give one geographic example of where each extreme occurs and explain a physical reason for that pattern.

### Question 2 - Hyetograph: Cumulative vs. Incremental

In one or two sentences, explain the difference between a cumulative rainfall plot and a hyetograph (incremental rainfall vs. time), and what each one tells you that the other does not.

### Question 3 - Areal Averaging: Arithmetic Mean vs. Thiessen Polygons

When you have several gauges in a watershed, the textbook describes two methods for averaging point rainfall to an areal value: the arithmetic mean and Thiessen polygons. In one or two sentences, explain how the Thiessen method differs and when it is more appropriate.

### Question 4 - IDF Curves and NOAA Atlas 14

In one or two sentences, explain what an Intensity-Duration-Frequency (IDF) curve shows and how an engineer uses one when selecting a design rainfall for a culvert or storm drain.
