# Pre-Class - Weather

This lesson covers the meteorology behind precipitation: how the atmosphere carries ocean evaporation over the continents, the atmospheric parameters (pressure, temperature, vapor pressure, humidity and dew point) tied together by the ideal gas law, the lifting mechanisms that produce clouds and storms, and how comparing lapse rates tells you whether the atmosphere is stable or unstable.

## Reading

## Pre-Class Questions

### Question 1 - Why study meteorology in a hydrology course?

Precipitation is the main input to the hydrologic cycle. In one or two sentences, explain how the atmosphere connects ocean evaporation to continental precipitation, and why an engineer who designs water systems needs to understand basic weather processes.

### Question 2 - Atmospheric Parameters and the Ideal Gas Law

State the ideal gas law in the form used for atmospheric air (equation 1-1), identify each variable with its units, and give one consequence of the relationship for warm versus cold air at the same pressure.

### Question 3 - Dew Point vs. Air Temperature

In one or two sentences, explain what dew point temperature tells you about the air that air temperature alone does not, and what physically happens at the dew point.

### Question 4 - Lifting Mechanisms

The textbook describes three principal lifting mechanisms that drive precipitation. Name them and give one short physical example or setting where each typically dominates.

### Question 5 - Lapse Rates and Atmospheric Stability

Define the dry adiabatic lapse rate and the environmental (ambient) lapse rate, and explain, in one or two sentences, how comparing the two tells you whether the atmosphere is stable or unstable to vertical motion.
